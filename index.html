Time Value of Money
Time Value of Money
A dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future — it can be invested to earn a return
Present Value (PV)
The value of a future sum of money in today's dollars. Moves money backward in time (discounting)
Future Value (FV)
The value of a current sum at a specific future date. Moves money forward in time (compounding)
Compounding
Growing money forward by applying the rate of return — multiplication. Calculates FV
Discounting
Shrinking future money back to today's value — division. Calculates PV
Discount Rate (r)
The return expected from an investment — used in discounting. Often set equal to WACC
Timeline
Visual tool for TVM scenarios. Cash inflows are positive; outflows are negative. Periods must be equal
Annuity Due
Payments made at the BEGINNING of each period
Ordinary Annuity
Payments made at the END of each period (most common)
NPV Rule
Invest in all independent projects with NPV > 0. For mutually exclusive projects, choose highest NPV
Corporate Finance & Risk
Key Roles
Corporate Finance
Sourcing and allocating funds for a corporation — short and long term
CFO
Head of finance — manages fiscal functions, monitors financial assets, develops forward-looking strategy
Shareholders
Own common stock; vote on major company matters; primary goal = maximizing wealth
Risk Management
Risk
Probability of a deviation from the anticipated outcome. Goal of risk management: increase firm value
Risk Threshold
The amount of risk a firm will tolerate — must be balanced against materiality (cost vs. benefit)
Capital Market
Where corporations and institutions trade financial instruments to fund long-term growth
Primary Market
Where new securities are issued directly by corporations to institutional investors (IPO)
Secondary Market
Where existing shareholders trade securities with other investors (e.g., NYSE, NASDAQ)
SWOT Analysis
Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — framework for assessing competitive position
SMART Goals
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Timely
Cost of Capital, CAPM & WACC
Cost of Debt
Cost of Debt
Effective interest rate a company pays on borrowed funds = Interest Expense / Debt
After-Tax Cost of Debt
C_bt × (1 − T). Interest is tax-deductible, reducing the true cost. Dividends are NOT tax-deductible
Risk-Free Rate (r_f)
Minimum return investors accept for zero-risk lending. Equated to US Treasury bond yields
Credit Risk
Risk that borrower defaults. Higher credit risk → higher cost of debt. Mitigated by collateral
Cost of Equity & CAPM
Cost of Equity
Minimum return stockholders require before investing. Higher than cost of debt due to greater risk
CAPM
Capital Asset Pricing Model — estimates cost of equity: Rₑ = r_f + β × (r_m − r_f)
Beta (β)
Measures stock volatility relative to the overall market. β > 1 = more volatile than market
Market Risk Premium
(r_m − r_f) — the extra return investors expect for taking on market risk vs. risk-free rate
WACC & Capital Structure
Capital Structure
The balance between debt and equity funding. High debt = highly leveraged
WACC
Weighted Average Cost of Capital — blended cost of all capital. A company must earn ROIC > WACC to create value
Tax Shield
Reduction in tax expense from deducting interest payments. More debt = greater tax shield
Distress Costs
Extra costs when a firm faces financial trouble — higher cost of capital, lost customers, talent loss
Modigliani-Miller Propositions
MM (No Taxes)
Prop I: D/E ratio is irrelevant to firm value. Prop II: WACC unaffected by capital structure
MM (With Taxes)
Prop I: Firm value maximized with maximum debt. Prop II: WACC minimized with all-debt structure
MM (Taxes + Bankruptcy)
Optimal structure: weighted cost of debt = weighted cost of equity. Balances tax shield vs. distress costs
Levered vs. Unlevered
Levered (geared): mix of debt + equity. Unlevered (ungeared): funded solely by equity
Capital Budgeting
Capital Budgeting
Using DCF analysis to determine which long-term investments are the best use of capital
NPV Rule
Invest in all independent projects with positive NPV. For mutually exclusive projects, choose highest NPV
IRR
Internal Rate of Return — the discount rate that makes NPV = $0. If IRR > required rate, NPV is positive
Payback Period
Time to recoup the initial investment. Simple but ignores time value of money — not a primary metric
Sunk Cost
Past costs already spent and unrecoverable — always irrelevant to capital budgeting decisions
Salvage Value
Resale value of project's assets at end of useful life — included in final period cash flows
Marginal Cash Flows
Future cash flows resulting from moving forward with a project
Incremental Cash Flows
Cash flows that occur ONLY IF the project is chosen — the relevant flows for NPV
Depreciation Treatment
Included to calculate tax expense (reduces taxable income), then added back as a non-cash item
Uncertainty Tools
Sensitivity Analysis
Shows how NPV changes when one critical variable changes — tests robustness
Break-even Analysis
How many units must be sold for NPV = $0 — minimum viable scale
Monte Carlo Simulation
Models all possible outcomes with probabilities and correlations — gives a distribution of NPVs
Value Creation & Stock Market
Value (of a firm)
The most an investor should pay — calculated as PV of all future free cash flows
ROIC
Return on Invested Capital — the return a company earns on its invested capital. Must exceed WACC to create value
Free Cash Flow
Cash available to return to investors (dividends, debt payments, buybacks) after capital expenditures
CAPEX
Capital Expenditures — money spent to fund growth activities (new factories, equipment, acquisitions)
Investment Rate
Fraction of cash flow reinvested into the business = Growth Rate / ROIC
Value Innovation
Simultaneously pursuing radically superior value for buyers AND lower costs for the company
Bolt-on Acquisition
Small company acquired and easily integrated into parent — a common growth lever
Market Cap
A company's total market value = Price per Share × Shares Outstanding
TRS
Total Return to Shareholders — stock price appreciation + dividends, expressed as annualized rate
Share Repurchase
Company buys back its own shares, reducing shares outstanding → immediately boosts stock price
Expectations Treadmill
Analysts set higher targets over time — company must keep exceeding them to maintain stock price
Short-termism
Dangerous focus on short-run results at the expense of long-term value creation
